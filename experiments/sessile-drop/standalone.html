Model, assumptions and literature

The shape is the small-drop spherical-cap limit of Young–Laplace: a sphere clipped by a plane, with fixed volume and specified contact angle. Gravity is diagnosed by Bond numbers, not solved. Surface roughness, pinning, Cassie/Wenzel transitions, motion and evaporation are not simulated.

Air has n=1; the support has a chosen n=1.52. Water’s wavelength-dependent index uses IAPWS R9-97 at 20°C and a nominal density of 998.207 kg/m³. Each curved or planar crossing obeys vector Snell refraction and Fresnel weights. Polarization intensities are tracked separately for the meridional paths. Multiple partial-reflection images, finite-aperture blur, interference, diffraction and environmental lighting are omitted. The rainbow sketch traces its own one-reflection branch.

Central magnification is the signed, local ray-map limit. A sign change indicates inversion; values near the singular limit are not a realistic prediction of infinite resolution. The grid uses a bounded radial lookup and raster sampling. This is a numerical reference study, not measured material fidelity or a new production renderer.

  • O’Brien & van den Brule (1991): small sessile-drop shapes, including angles above 90°.
  • van der Heijden et al. (2018): spherical-cap geometry, wetting and pinning.
  • Kilbride et al. (2023): pattern distortion, droplet shape and magnification.
  • IAPWS R9-97: refractive index versus wavelength, temperature and density.
  • IAPWS: surface tension formulation.
